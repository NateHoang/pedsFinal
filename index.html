Lecture 8: Oral Path and Anomalies
Abnormality Category Key Features / Description Management / Treatment
Bohn's Nodules Oral Pathology (Soft Tissue, Infancy)
  • White to yellow-white nodules ~1–3 mm, clustered
  • Located on buccal and lingual alveolar ridge
  • Trapped salivary gland remnants
  • Occurs in 55–85% of neonates
 No treatment; self-resolves by rupturing or resorption
Epstein's Pearls Oral Pathology (Soft Tissue, Infancy)
  • White to yellow-white nodules ~1–3 mm, clustered
  • Located on midline of palate
  • Trapped epithelial remnants
  • Occurs in 55–85% of neonates
 No treatment; self-resolves by rupturing or resorption
Dental Lamina Cysts Oral Pathology (Soft Tissue, Infancy)
  • Collective term for Bohn's Nodules and Epstein's Pearls
  • Similar appearance, histology and treatment
  • Slight differences in location and origin
 No treatment; self-resolves
Congenital Epulis Oral Pathology (Soft Tissue, Infancy)
  • Pink to red polypoid mass, typically ~2 mm
  • Maxillary alveolar ridge (rarely tongue)
  • Suspected neural mesenchymal growth
  • Rare (>1%), more common in females
 Surgical excision (may interfere with feeding and breathing)
Riga Fede Oral Pathology (Soft Tissue, Infancy)
  • Ulceration with fibrinopurulent membrane
  • Ventral tongue (small % dorsal)
  • Trauma from erupting teeth
  • Age 1 week–1 year
  • 1 in 2000–3500
 Remove irritant by smoothing or removal; rapid healing
Melanotic Neuroectodermal Tumor of Infancy Oral Pathology (Soft Tissue, Infancy)
  • Pigmented neoplasm; rapidly growing, expansile but benign
  • Maxilla 62%, skull 16%, mandible 8%
  • From neural crest cells
  • Presents in first year of life
  • Extremely rare (<0.1%)
 Surgical excision required; possible recurrence
Eruption Cyst / Hematoma Oral Pathology (Soft Tissue, Infancy)
  • Soft, translucent blue-gray swelling over an erupting tooth (darker if traumatized)
  • Dental follicle fills with fluid
  • During eruption (primary and permanent)
  • More common in maxilla, central incisors, permanent first molars
  • Often self-resolves
  • Surgical opening if lesion persists to facilitate eruption
Natal / Neonatal Teeth Anomaly of Number
  • Natal = tooth present at birth; Neonatal = present within 30 days
  • 85% mandibular
  • 90% normal primary incisor, 10% supernumerary
  • Can impede nursing, cause Riga Fede, aspiration risk
 Smooth rough edges, monitor, or extract
Fusion Anomaly of Number
  • Developing tooth buds join with hard tissue union
  • Larger-than-expected tooth with two distinct pulp chambers
  • One less tooth in the arch
  • Plaque-retentive, caries-prone, can impede exfoliation/eruption
  • Restoration/seal fusion line
  • Extraction of primary fusions
  • Esthetic restoration of permanent fusions
Gemination Anomaly of Number
  • Single tooth bud partially divides
  • Split crowns with single root/canal
  • Larger crown but normal tooth count
  • Plaque-retentive and can cause crowding
  • Restoration/seal
  • Extraction
  • Esthetic restoration of permanent teeth
Supernumerary Teeth Anomaly of Number
  • Complete extra tooth
  • Prevalence 0.1–3.8%; more common in permanent dentition
  • Most often a single extra (~75%)
  • Maxillary incisors most common
  • Mesiodens = supernumerary at maxillary midline
  • Can prevent eruption, cause resorption, increase crowding
Monitor, removal, orthodontic treatment
Dens Evaginatus Abnormality of Shape
  • Accessory cusp/tubercle forms, often with an associated pulp horn
  • More common in permanent teeth, lateral incisors and mandibular second premolars
  • Often an occlusal interference that wears quickly, leading to pulp necrosis without caries
  • Slowly grind tubercle, reinforce with composite, or remove with pulp cap
  • Endodontic therapy if pulp involved
Dens Invaginatus (Dens in dente) Abnormality of Shape
  • Lingual pit overextends, creating a channel into the tooth
  • More common in permanent teeth; permanent lateral most common
  • Often bilateral
  • Difficult-to-clean area; pit routes bacteria toward pulp
  • Prophylactic filling/seal of the channel
  • Endodontic therapy as needed
Taurodontism Abnormality of Shape
  • Long pulp chambers and shorter roots
  • Most often permanent molars
  • Caused by failure of Hertwig's epithelial root sheath to invaginate
  • Large pulps complicate endodontics; root form complicates periodontal therapy
Maintain vitality
Amelogenesis Imperfecta (Types I–IV) Congenital Anomaly
  • Group of hereditary disorders of defective enamel
  • Type I hypoplastic: less enamel, normal quality
  • Type II hypomaturation: normal amount, immature and chips easily
  • Type III hypocalcification: normal shape, minimal mineral, very soft, yellow/orange to brown/black
  • Type IV hypoplastic + taurodontism
  • Teeth develop caries easily; sensitive from exposed dentin
  • Full coverage restorations preferred
  • Manage comorbidities (ortho for open bites, endo for pulp, prosthodontic rehab for lost vertical dimension)
  • Maintain dentition
Dentinogenesis Imperfecta / Dysplasia Congenital Anomaly
  • Hereditary disorders of dentin formation/quality affecting both dentitions (primary more affected)
  • DI Type I associated with osteogenesis imperfecta
  • DI Type II pulpal obliteration
  • DI Type III shell teeth (pulp enlargement)
  • Dentin Dysplasia I rootless teeth
  • Prophylactic full coverage restorations in primary dentition
  • Pulp therapy often required but difficult
  • Extractions and prosthetics
  • Maintain space
Hypophosphatasia Congenital Anomaly
  • Inherited deficiency of tissue alkaline phosphatase
  • Defects of mineralized tissue; cementum most affected
  • Cementum defects lead to early loss of primary teeth and possible inappropriate permanent tooth loss without root resorption
  • Maintain space in primary dentition
  • Esthetic replacement of lost incisors
  • Assess permanent roots
  • Prosthetic replacement as appropriate
Drug Staining Acquired Color Defect
  • Medications with strong pigmentation incorporated into developing dental tissues
  • Color typically banded and follows chronology of tooth development
  • Hard tissues otherwise normal
 Cosmetic/esthetic considerations (hard tissues normal)
Hyperbilirubinemia Acquired Color Defect
  • Excess bilirubin (before and after birth) incorporated into dental hard tissues
  • Causes green-blue discoloration
 —
Porphyria Acquired Color Defect
  • Hereditary defective porphyrin metabolism
  • Excess porphyrin deposited into dentition
  • Teeth red-brown with UV fluorescence
  • Usually more pronounced in primary teeth
 —
Congenital Syphilis Acquired Structure Defect
  • Impacts tooth development
  • Hutchinson incisors: narrow, tapered incisors with hypoplastic notch
  • Mulberry molars: tapered molars with constricted occlusal table and atypical globular anatomy
  • Anomalies persist even after infection treated
 —
Rickets Acquired Structure Defect
  • Vitamin D deficiency (rare in developed countries)
  • May cause hypoplasia in teeth developing during the time of deficiency
—
Vitamin D Resistant Rickets Acquired Structure Defect
  • Hereditary condition with rickets symptoms despite adequate Vitamin D
  • Tooth hypoplasia and more notable structural anomalies
  • Large pulp chambers with elongated pulp horns
  • Frequent pulp necrosis and abscess
 —
Turner Hypoplasia Acquired Structure Defect
  • Necrotic primary teeth provide a pathway for bacterial infections to reach developing permanent teeth
  • Developing teeth subjected to bacterial byproducts develop irregularly
—
Molar Incisor Hypomineralization (MIH) Acquired Structure Defect
  • Disruptions around birth cause hypomineralization on first permanent molars and incisors
  • All other teeth normal
  • Pattern rarely linear; severity varies tooth to tooth
  • Teeth sensitive and caries-prone
  • Monitoring
  • Fluoride varnish and sealants
  • Restorations
  • Early full coverage crowns
  • Extraction and second molar substitution
Molar Root Incisor Malformation (MRIM) Acquired Structure Defect
  • Serious medical issues in the first 2 years of life can impact the roots of permanent first molars, incisors, and sometimes primary second molars
  • Teeth prone to spontaneous necrosis
  • Extremely difficult to treat endodontically
 Extraction and second molar substitution
Fluorosis Acquired Structure Defect
  • Excess fluoride during development leads to irregular enamel crystal deposition
  • Enamel from opaque white to mottled brown
  • Primary dentition often unaffected
  • Resistant to decalcification but surface irregularities collect plaque
  • Unesthetic
  • Monitoring for minor cases
  • Esthetic restoration
